    Hanogi Mata Temple

    Old NH3 near Kun village, between Pandoh and Aut

    4.6Google rating

    Drivers on this road traditionally stopped here to ask for safe passage. The shrine sits on a cliff above the Beas, with a swaying footbridge below. The new 12.5 km twin tunnel now bypasses it, and stretches of the old road were shut after monsoon damage in 2024. Only go if people at Pandoh tell you the old road is open.

    Photo tipWalk out onto the footbridge for the gorge shot. The steps up to the temple take about 15 minutes.

    Larji Confluence

    Larji bridge, where the Sainj and Tirthan meet the Beas

    Three rivers, one reservoir

    Right after your coffee, you cross the Beas on the Larji bridge. The Sainj and Tirthan flow into the reservoir here, and on a still afternoon the water turns into a mirror. It’s also where the Sainj Valley road begins, so it makes a good marker for the last leg.

OLD-SCHOOL OPTION

Teja Guest House

Run by Tej Ram and his wife. It’s a simple local homestay that travel writers describe as closer to the meadow than the hostels. Call directly: +91 98167 31367. There’s no website, so confirm the rate and parking on the phone.

About the last stretch: Sainj to Shangarh is about 15 km of narrow road with rough patches, and you’ll do it close to dusk. Tell your host your ETA from Aut, since Google Maps is unreliable in the valley and most hosts send a pin or meet you at the Ropa turn-off. Walking times are as described by the properties and their guests.

Why not Murthal?

At 05:00 you'll pass Murthal around 06:15. That's too early for a real coffee stop and too soon to need one, so save your appetite for Kurukshetra.

After Aut

The Sainj–Shangarh stretch is single-lane in places. Try to be past Sainj before dark, and fill up at Aut: fuel pumps get scarce after that.

Post-monsoon check

It's the end of September, so check HP road status for the Mandi–Aut and Sainj stretches the evening before you leave.
